# Manual Answer Test — Round 1

## Purpose and status

**Unrun experiment in Product Discovery / Problem Validation.** Test whether a concise risk-oriented answer helps a real person resolve a packing decision beyond a conventional weather summary and packing list. This document contains no participant results or validation-success claim.

Authority and provenance:

- [Product blueprint](../product-blueprint.md) and [Product Validation baseline](product-validation-baseline.md): current product intent and accepted validation boundaries, including asymmetric mistakes and the higher burden for omission advice.
- [Community evidence](community-evidence-corpus.md): observed questions and constraints, not weather authority or proof that these answers work.
- [Scenario corpus](representative-scenario-corpus.md): synthetic fixtures anchored in those questions, not observed trips.
- **Experiment format:** the cards, wording, sample size, questions, and interpretation rules below are provisional research choices.
- **Hypothesis:** explicit risk, coverage, and uncertainty explanations improve a concrete decision or grounded confidence. Neither that hypothesis nor these labels are accepted product requirements. A later explicit product decision would be needed to adopt them.

## Small scenario subset

Use four cases; each person receives one relevant case and both answer conditions. These cover distinct decisions without running all twelve scenarios. Winter minimum sets, footwear trade-offs, and extended laundry cycles remain outside this first round; findings cannot establish full corpus coverage.

| Fixture / evidence anchors | Decision the person must resolve | What this case tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S01 — warm-climate traveler, late March–April, two cities, reluctant to buy dedicated gear (E03, E04) | Pack/buy a heavy winter coat, use a lighter outer layer plus removable insulation, or defer that choice pending specific information? | Costly underpacking versus unnecessary bulk; whole-trip coverage and uncertainty. |
| S02 — several weeks in Hamburg in June, host-family laundry, 8 kg bag versus extra 23 kg bag (E02) | Does the proposed clothing set justify an extra checked bag, or can clothing fit the small-bag plan? | Whether laundry and removing redundancy change the luggage decision. Record non-clothing space as unknown unless supplied; do not infer total baggage sufficiency from clothing alone. |
| S04 — early September, Berlin + Munich, mixed tops, pants/skirts, cardigan and light jacket already packed (E01) | Add a heavier layer or keep the existing set; which existing layer is worn on departure and carried for later? | Avoiding redundant additions and distinguishing suitcase contents from day-bag contents. |
| S09 — first winter exposure, destination city unsettled (E14) | What shoe/outerwear capabilities can be identified now, and which purchase or omission must wait for destination and exposure details? | Useful partial answers without false city-specific certainty; an explicit “cannot yet decide” can be the correct outcome. |

For S02, leave the separate boots question out of the primary test. Do not add every adjacent question to the session.

## Prepare one paired answer sheet per case

Start with 4–8 consenting adults unfamiliar with German weather, aiming for 1–2 per case. This is a convenience sample for finding decision signals and failures, not a statistical validation threshold. Prefer an actual upcoming trip with the matching decision. Otherwise use the fixture and mark all choices as hypothetical. Do not recruit or contact anyone automatically.

Before showing answers, freeze a short shared input sheet:

- Fixture ID, any participant-provided adaptations, cities/date range or explicit unknowns, indoor/outdoor exposure, and time remaining before travel.
- Current clothing options and their relevant capabilities, luggage constraint and laundry access. Ask about cold sensitivity only where it could change the choice; log whether it did.
- A common conditions brief covering relevant trip/day variation, plus missing information. Both answers must use the same brief and uncertainty.

**Two evidence modes:** For an actual trip, verify weather/climate claims through the repository's [docs-first policy](../../.codex/rules/docs-first-policy.md) and [research skill](../../.agents/skills/docs-first-research/SKILL.md). Record source URL, retrieval date, location/time coverage and limitations beside the brief. Do not use community replies as weather authority or invent a universal forecast cutoff. For a fixture session, explicitly label any added conditions “assumed for this exercise; not a forecast or travel recommendation.” Never pass invented temperatures or conditions off as real evidence. Unknown inputs may stay unknown.

Write and freeze the pair before the session; assign neutral labels A/B and keep the condition key in facilitator notes. Aim for 120–180 words per answer with comparable reading time. This length is an experiment convenience, not a product constraint. Preserve the pair and input sheet with a revision/date so observations refer to exact wording. A rehearsal may check clarity and fairness, but synthetic evaluations are preparation only.

### Provisional risk-oriented format

Use the same five short fields in every case; “not supported yet” is a valid field value:

1. **Main packing risk:** the specific mistake and its consequence for this trip.
2. **Must bring / cover:** the smallest supported capability set, mapped to existing items where possible, with a reason. Qualify or withhold if evidence is insufficient.
3. **Safe to omit / unresolved:** name the candidate bulky item and why its capability is already covered, or explicitly say omission is not supported yet. “Optional” is not permission to omit a necessary capability.
4. **Wear versus carry:** what is worn and what goes in the day bag for the stated exposure, or why this cannot yet be specified.
5. **Confidence and next check:** basis, important unknown, and what information or changed condition would alter the decision. Avoid unsupported numerical confidence.

**Omission gate:** Before writing “Safe to omit,” document the candidate item's function, the retained substitute, coverage across all known cities and meaningful exposures, relevant comfort needs, and the source basis/limitations. An average temperature, season label, absent rain mention, or synthetic assumption alone cannot justify real-trip omission. If any material coverage or evidence gap remains, write “Omission not established; check [specific missing input]” and retain a conditional choice. Do not silently recommend buying a replacement at the destination as the fallback. For fixtures, any omission conclusion is conditional within the exercise only. Even a source-backed rationale is a testable judgment, not a proven safety guarantee or an established numerical safety threshold.

### Conventional comparison condition

Give a readable weather/conditions summary followed by an ordinary clothing packing list, with a brief caveat for uncertainty. Use the same facts, candidate garments/capabilities, luggage/laundry constraints, and underlying advice as the risk-oriented answer. Include ordinary useful layering advice where warranted; do not make the comparison deliberately vague, exhaustive, or wrong. Express uncertain items as conditional in both versions.

The experimental difference is organization around the decision, explicit risk/redundancy reasoning, and wear/carry distinctions, rather than additional weather information or a better garment selection. Check factual and recommendation parity before testing; record any unavoidable difference as a confound. This is a manually authored comparison, not a test of a named competitor or evidence of market differentiation.

## Run a 20–25 minute session

Alternate which condition appears first within each case where possible. Record the order. With only one person in a case, order effects remain unresolved. Do not call either answer “better,” explain its intent, or coach choices before recording them.

1. **Before answers:** “What would you pack, leave behind, wear, or carry now? What is still undecided? What would getting this wrong cost you?” Record the exact focal choice, reason, and confidence from 1 (cannot decide) to 5 (ready to act). For a real trip, distinguish a plan from something already packed or purchased.
2. **After the first answer:** Ask the person to state their choice again, including retained/deferred choices, and repeat the confidence rating. Ask: “What, if anything, changed? Point to the sentence that caused it. What would you actually add, remove, buy, or carry?” Record before discussion.
3. **Comprehension and uncertainty:** “Why is that item needed or removable? What conditions might change your choice? What does this answer still not know? What would you check next?” In particular, ask whether omission applies to the entire trip and whether a carried layer must still be packed. Record their interpretation, not just assent.
4. **After the second answer:** Repeat steps 2–3, recording incremental changes separately. Then ask: “Which answer, if either, helped the decision, and how? Was anything misleading or missing? Would the first answer alone have been enough?” Preference alone is not an outcome.
5. **Close:** Explain fixture assumptions and unresolved advice before anyone uses the answer for travel. If the participant agrees to a later follow-up for an actual trip, ask whether they followed the choice and encountered unused bulk, missing coverage, an unplanned purchase, or changed conditions. Follow-up is optional; without it, record intended behavior only.

## Copyable session record

Use an anonymous session code. Do not commit names, contact details, recordings, identifying itineraries, or raw personal data. Keep only consented, de-identified observations or paraphrases; do not fabricate missing responses. No participant data is collected by creating this harness.

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Session / case / mode | Anonymous code; fixture ID; actual-trip or hypothetical; adaptations; relevant climate familiarity |
| Materials | Input-sheet and answer revisions; source basis or explicit assumptions; parity check; A/B condition key and order |
| Baseline | Concrete choice or unresolved fork; reason; confidence 1–5; consequence of error |
| After each condition | Exact added/removed/retained/deferred choice; confidence; causal sentence; participant's reason; time/help needed |
| Understanding | Retained capability; wear/carry distinction; understood limitation and trigger for reconsideration |
| Failure / uncertainty | Unsupported omission; missing coverage; redundant bulk; misunderstood wording; missing fact; facilitator intervention; order/factual confound |
| Outcome | Decision signal, grounded confidence signal, no added value, adverse signal, or inconclusive; justification |
| Follow-up, if any | Intended versus actual action; reported consequence; changed conditions; otherwise “not observed” |

## Interpret observations without declaring success

Use these provisional coding rules consistently; preserve disconfirming cases:

- **Decision signal:** a named, consequential packing/purchase/luggage/wear-carry choice changes, and the person can tie it to a reason in the answer. Record whether the change appears supported by the shared evidence; a change toward underpacking is adverse, not a win.
- **Grounded confidence signal:** the focal choice can stay the same, but confidence increases by at least two scale points, the person says this settles a previously blocking doubt, and they can explain coverage and limitations without coaching. This is a provisional materiality rule, not a validated metric. A higher score with misunderstood uncertainty is false-confidence evidence.
- **No added value:** preference for wording, a small unexplained score increase, repeating an already settled plan, or the conventional answer resolving the decision equally well. Increased confidence after both cards cannot by itself establish the second card's advantage.
- **Adverse signal:** leaving necessary coverage behind, adding unexplained bulk, reading “carry” as “do not pack,” interpreting unknowns as certainty, or treating conditional omission as universal. Flag for revision before reusing that answer, regardless of preference or score.
- **Inconclusive:** missing decision-relevant inputs, mismatched participant/case, leading prompts, unequal information, or order contamination preventing attribution. A justified deferral demonstrates an uncertainty boundary; it does not alone prove improved packing value.

After the round, write a short evidence summary: cases/people/modes/orders tested; per-condition choices and grounded confidence signals; equal or better conventional outcomes; adverse and inconclusive cases; follow-up availability; and the smallest next change or test. Separate real-trip intent, hypothetical choices, and observed post-trip behavior. Small counts describe this sample only.

Continue with a revised answer only where observations show a plausible benefit worth retesting. Narrow the hypothesis if value appears in just one case; reconsider the format if the comparison works as well; repair unsupported omission or false confidence before further use. Do not average away a consequential failure. This round cannot alone satisfy the baseline's combined success boundary, establish competitor differentiation, or authorize implementation. No app architecture, provider, AI approach, product code, or agent tooling is selected by this experiment.
